# Deep offline-RL path: an empirical and novelty audit

Date: 2026-09-29. Scope: trajectory deletion from a *fixed offline log*. This memo uses primary papers, official benchmark documentation, and authors' code. It is an audit for planning, not a claim that the proposed methods have already been validated.

## Executive decision

Make deep RL an **independent empirical test of a carefully scoped counterfactual deletion algorithm**, rather than calling the tabular Bellman certificate a neural guarantee. The most credible first GPU target is a fixed-feature or publicly pretrained encoder with an explicitly retrainable RL head, followed by full IQL as an empirical extension. A shared neural encoder learned on the full log already contains forget-data influence; downdating only its head cannot remove that influence. If the goal is full-model deletion, the encoder must be trained without deletable trajectories, retrained, or incorporated into a separate approximate method whose limitations are measured against scratch retraining.

The paper story that might survive review is: **trajectory deletion cost and fidelity depend on Bellman propagation and retained-data coverage, and a method can predict when cheap repair works, with a measured fallback when it does not**. The GPU experiments must show this prediction remains useful under approximation. Tabular count subtraction, warm starts, gradient ascent, Fisher weighting, and selective layer updates alone are not novel enough.

The direct data-influence target is the policy that the *specified training algorithm* would produce on the retained log, `A(D_retain)`. A method that assigns low probability or low Q to forgotten state-actions may be farther from that target: retained trajectories can support exactly the same action. Include a constructed overlap case where suppression and counterfactual retraining disagree.

## Why tabular locality does not transfer mechanically

1. The tabular proof assumes an exact Bellman optimality operator on a finite empirical MDP with discount contraction. IQL and CQL jointly optimize neural actor and critic losses under function approximation; projected Bellman operators need not be sup-norm contractions. A low residual measured only on logged states says little about unseen or successor states.
2. A tabular row change is local in stored sufficient statistics. A neural gradient step on one trajectory changes shared parameters and potentially every state-action prediction. Bootstrapped targets and target networks can amplify the spread. A "predecessor graph" built with nearest neighbors in latent space is a heuristic, not an exact dependency graph.
3. Learned observation normalization, encoders, actor heads, critics, target networks, replay sampling state, and optimizer moments can each carry forget-data influence. Define the released artifact precisely. A policy-only guarantee must not be called full checkpoint removal.
4. Even a fixed-feature linear critic needs care in *control*. For policy evaluation, feature cross-products can be downdated. In fitted control, a changed policy or max/expectile target changes targets for retained samples, so a one-shot matrix downdate is not the whole update.
5. "Close to one retrained checkpoint" is noisy when optimization is stochastic. Use paired seeds/initialization for fidelity, and multiple independent retrains to estimate the reference distribution. Report whether no-op is already indistinguishable from retraining at the chosen deletion size; if so, that split has low power to establish improvement.

## Feasible algorithm family and falsifiable hypotheses

**Bridge A: clean frozen features + counterfactual head repair.** Use random fixed features or an encoder pretrained on a separate public/non-deletable dataset. Never fit it or its normalization on the potentially deleted log. For fixed-policy evaluation, store per-trajectory sufficient statistics for LSTD/regularized linear Bellman regression and downdate them on deletion. Re-solve when conditioning warrants. For control, implement a declared finite fitted-Q/policy-iteration schedule and recompute the retained targets to convergence or a specified tolerance. A local solver can prioritize changed targets but must measure the cost of a *global* verification scan or exact linear solve. This is a deep representation plus mathematically honest deletion target, but its deployment claim is limited to this architecture.

**Bridge B: full IQL empirical repair.** Start with the same full-data IQL checkpoint, delete complete episodes, and use retained-only critic/value/actor updates. Prioritize retained minibatches based on a deletion-induced target discrepancy or a fitted influence score, but allocate a fixed exploration fraction uniformly across retained data to avoid blind spots. Recompute or validate priorities after each phase. Stop via a held-out retained Bellman/actor consistency diagnostic plus observed parameter/policy drift; call this an *empirical stopping rule*, not a certificate. Fallback to full retained-data fine-tuning or retraining when diagnostics fail. Any actor/critic or encoder refit must be included in deletion cost. A "local" minibatch sampler with no measurable advantage over uniform retained fine-tuning is not a contribution.

Three pre-registered hypotheses:

- **H1, locality prediction:** coverage loss, successor/predecessor reachability in the tabular surrogate, and action-gap fragility predict the *measured* correction work and retraining-relative policy error better than forget-set size alone. Test across random, rare-coverage, high-return, and bottleneck trajectory strata. The hypothesis fails if predictive rank correlation or held-out error improvement over size-only controls is negligible.
- **H2, fidelity at fixed compute:** adaptive repair is closer to retained-data retraining in action distribution and multi-attack audit than budget-matched retained fine-tuning, GA+Refit, and TrajDeleter, without losing retained return. It fails if a strong fine-tune matches it at equal GPU-seconds or if the gain appears only on one privacy score.
- **H3, honest fallback:** detected hard deletions trigger broader recomputation, yielding a better compute–fidelity frontier than always-local or always-full updating. It fails if fallback overhead makes it slower than warm-started full update or if the trigger misses hard cases.

Do not introduce the neural method as "certified" unless a separate theorem covers the complete released learner, including the encoder and stochastic training choices. If only Bridge A succeeds, frame the paper around a restricted but rigorous learner, with IQL results as a transfer stress test.

## Benchmark design

**Dataset identity.** For a direct TOUR/TrajDeleter comparison, use the same original D4RL `*-v2` snapshots and environment normalization. [Farama's D4RL repository](https://github.com/Farama-Foundation/D4RL) says the project moved to Minari, but [Minari's D4RL documentation](https://minari.farama.org/datasets/D4RL/index.html) explicitly says some datasets are reproductions and are *not identical* to original D4RL. Minari is attractive for new episode-aware experiments, but run it as a separate dataset track and label its IDs/versions. Never pool D4RL and Minari scores as if the datasets were interchangeable. Save dataset hash, episode-boundary/timeout rule, and exact deletion IDs.

**Progression.** Pilot on one locomotion dataset with a nontrivial no-op-versus-retrain signal; HalfCheetah medium-expert is a reasonable candidate because TOUR reports a visible base/retrain forget-gap difference there, whereas some Hopper/Walker2D slices have weak signal. Confirm with our own training. Then cover HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2D on medium and medium-expert; medium-replay is a coverage stress test. AntMaze is a later generalization test with a separate success-rate metric. Reserve one environment–variant and a set of deletion IDs as the final holdout after tuning.

**Splits.** Split by complete episode, not transition. Pilot 10% uniformly random episode deletion to align with TOUR. Add predeclared 1%, 5%, 10%, and 20% deletion sizes; high-return, rare-coverage, and bottleneck/bridge strata. High-return requests are intrinsically distribution-shifting, so compare each forget set against matched retained/non-member episodes and report coverage and return before interpreting membership. For user-level requests, group all episodes by user ID and remove the whole block; D4RL generally lacks meaningful user identity, so this needs a separately collected or synthetic user-tagged dataset.

**Learners.** Choose IQL as the first actor–critic family using the [authors' implementation](https://github.com/Improbable-AI/harness-offline-rl/tree/master/implicit_q_learning), or a validated modern port with identical objective. Add CQL through its [authors' implementation](https://github.com/aviralkumar2907/CQL) only after IQL demonstrates signal. Decision Transformer via the [authors' code](https://github.com/kzl/decision-transformer) is valuable for reproducing TOUR's exact likelihood audit, but DT is a separate learner family. Every deletion method and its retrain target must use the *same* family, architecture, preprocessing and training budget.

**Required baselines within each learner:** original/no-op; scratch retrain on retained episodes; uniform retained-only fine-tuning; warm-started full retained update to the same convergence budget; GA+retain refit; TrajDeleter; the proposed prioritization without adaptive fallback; the proposed method with fallback. Add RFF only for diffusion policies. Match *wall-clock or gradient-step budgets* as well as final accuracy; a method that uses more precomputation must report it. Include an over-forgetting example to expose Q suppression when retained evidence supports the same action.

**Metrics.** Primary: deployment return/success on fixed evaluation seeds; action-distribution distance to paired retrain on a held-out retained-state set; retained and forget trajectory action losses; critic-value or Bellman-target discrepancy against retrain; total deletion wall-clock, GPU-seconds, peak VRAM, precomputation/storage and fallback frequency. For stochastic policies, estimate symmetric KL or Jensen–Shannon distance with clear sampling; for deterministic policies, action MSE plus downstream return. Use *separate* held-out environment initial states to avoid evaluating only logged states.

Privacy audit: paired forget-versus-matched-nonmember AUC, retain-versus-nonmember AUC, retrain-relative excess leakage, and at least two attack families (e.g. action error/likelihood and reference-model or query-limited shadow attack). TOUR warns that `|AUC-0.5|` can mislead without retain utility and matched controls; the retrain reference itself need not have AUC 0.5. Compare attacks on all baselines and include a collapsed-policy negative control. A favorable attack result is evidence under that threat model, not proof of exact removal.

**Statistics.** Three independent training seeds and two predeclared deletion requests per setting suffice for a pilot; final central claims need at least five independent training seeds and multiple fixed deletion requests per stratum, with hierarchical uncertainty over seeds and requests. Treat thousands of time steps in one episode as correlated. Pair method runs on identical retained sets, initializations where applicable, evaluation seeds and hardware. Report per-environment scatter as well as means and intervals. Do not tune on the held-out task/deletion IDs.

## GPU schedule and stop rules

TOUR reports about 15–25 minutes for one 100K-step DT training run on a 16 GB RTX 4070 Ti SUPER and ran three-seed experiments on that single GPU ([paper, Appendix A.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21111v1)); this is **not** an IQL runtime estimate. Measure one base and one retrain end to end on the user's GPU before booking a matrix. A minimal pilot of three training seeds, two deletion requests, one task and four update methods requires three base runs, six retrains and 24 update runs, plus audit/rollout time. At TOUR's DT rate this would be roughly 8–14 training GPU-hours *if all runs cost one DT training*, an unrealistic upper/lower assumption for unequal update budgets. Record actual time per method and recalculate before expansion.

1. **Week 1:** freeze dataset identity, episode parser, hashes, algorithm and evaluation manifest. Reproduce original and retained retrain returns; verify no-op and deliberate collapse are separable in at least one fidelity/attack metric.
2. **Weeks 2–3:** implement matched splits, two attacks, paired policy-distance/return, and budget-matched fine-tuning. This must work before adding the new method.
3. **Weeks 4–5:** port or run TrajDeleter; verify key loss terms and one task against authors' setup. Build Bridge A and its exact retained reference; check encoder/normalization provenance.
4. **Weeks 6–7:** develop Bridge B and ablate priority signal, uniform sampling fraction, actor versus critic repair, and fallback. Discard the method if ordinary fine-tuning dominates at fixed compute.
5. **Weeks 8–10:** expand to three locomotion tasks and at least five seeds only after pilot gates pass. Keep medium-replay/AntMaze as separate stress tests. Audit every headline result under multiple attacks.
6. **Weeks 11–12:** preregister a final holdout matrix, run it once, report negative cases and total GPU-hours, then write claims no stronger than observed evidence.

Allocate roughly one third of pilot GPU time to scratch retained retrains: they are the counterfactual target and calibrate all other results. If that cost is too high, reduce environment breadth before reducing retrain seeds. Use checkpoint reuse only where it does not contaminate the scratch reference. Do not quote a speedup until the numerator and denominator include preprocessing, index construction amortization, deletion update, convergence/fallback and necessary policy validation under the same hardware.

## Publication risk

The most serious risk is that all deep gains disappear against well-tuned retained-only fine-tuning, which TOUR already found can be strong. The second is that a "locality" score merely correlates with deletion size or trajectory reward and offers no held-out prediction. The third is using an encoder trained on forget data while claiming the head downdate removes all influence. The fourth is treating membership AUC near 0.5 as success when utility has collapsed or retraining itself has nonzero AUC.

The strongest outcome would combine (i) a formal restricted-learner theorem for a clean fixed representation, (ii) a measured predictor of hard versus easy deletion, (iii) a compute-aware fallback, and (iv) full actor–critic results that approach retraining across multiple attacks and tasks. If (iv) fails, report the boundary plainly; a rigorous negative result about neural locality may still be valuable but would need a strong mechanistic explanation and broad reproducibility.
